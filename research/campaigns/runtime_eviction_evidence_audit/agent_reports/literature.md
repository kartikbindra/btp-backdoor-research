# Literature and evidence audit

## Objective

Verify CacheTrap and StripWake against their primary papers and check the manuscript's comparison against the experimental record. The scope is evidence and attribution.

## Findings

### CacheTrap

- The gray-box Trojan operates on a victim LLM with a public same-architecture surrogate. The attacker has no access to the victim's exact parameters, training data, or gradients and assumes direct transient cache corruption at inference. The paper studies a single altered bit in a cached value, with clean victim task fine-tuning preceding evaluation and no adversarial modification to the victim checkpoint or input. Its central similarity to this project is the text KV-cache surface. The distinguishing causal variable is a deliberately corrupted value versus an operator's ordinary eviction policy acting on a trained checkpoint. [CacheTrap §III and Fig. 2; §IV and §V](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.22681v3)
- The main table reports per-target-class ASR across ARC-Easy, ARC-Challenge, OpenBookQA, and TREC for LLaMA-2-7B-Chat, LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.1, Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B. ASR is the share of test samples classified as a specified target when corruption is applied. The stated denominator is the target test set, with no ground-truth target-class exclusion stated in that definition and no per-cell count in Table II. Values span 97.07%–100%. Accuracy on clean-cache samples matches the ordinary clean baseline, a utility claim for the condition without corruption. Table II reports the best candidate on the evaluation set, so its entries are not directly comparable to a frozen, one-shot evaluation. Table IV separately shows same-architecture transfer on two fine-tuned victim tasks. [CacheTrap §§IV-B.4, V-A–B, Tables II and IV](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.22681v3)
- Evidence is organized from threat model to benchmark table, transfer and sensitivity evidence, then a separate hardware feasibility evaluation. The hardware section does not make every Table II result a hardware demonstration. CacheTrap also discusses hardware defenses in §VI, so describing all neighbors as attack-only would overstate the distinction. [CacheTrap §§V-C and VI](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.22681v3)

### StripWake

- The adversary controls projector training and releases a compromised multimodal checkpoint. The vision encoder and language model stay frozen. Evaluation requires a trigger-bearing image, and ordinary inference-time pruning of visual patch tokens removes surrounding context. It tests actual compressors only at inference and assumes the attacker does not know the downstream compressor or budget. Some compressors operate before the LLM and others inside it. The projector is the trained component. Our text study uses the same unmodified user prompt under full and pruned cache conditions and assumes the attacker can broadly anticipate the deployment policy. The direct conceptual overlap is dormant behavior under a full-context audit and activation after a legitimate inference transformation. [StripWake §§1, 3, 5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.01252v1)
- LLaVA-1.5-7B is the sole evaluated model. The default is 500 held-out COCO Karpathy images across three training seeds, reporting mean ± standard deviation. ASR is exact target-string containment in greedy outputs from trigger-bearing images. Clean utility is captioning CIDEr on clean inputs, with changes given relative to the clean model under each reported policy. Table 3 shows 0.4% ± 0.2% at full tokens and 94.0%–99.7% for the five primary methods at 64 of 576 tokens. Two supplementary pruning methods are in that table. Table 4 adds four unseen compressors, with the weaker FlowCut result at that budget (79.8% ± 3.9%) and 97.7%–99.7% across all four at 128 tokens. CIDEr changes are −0.4, −0.4, and +1.1 points for full tokens, CLS pruning, and FastV respectively. This describes utility on clean inputs and says nothing about retaining a useful caption in a targeted response. [StripWake §§5.1, 5.3, Tables 3–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.01252v1)
- The paper uses a budget/qualitative figure, mechanism interventions, a primary comparison with an unpoisoned matched model and two attack baselines, then transfer, ablations and appendix checks. Figure 1 uses 300 triggered images. Appendix C Table 7 uses 300 held-out images with bootstrap intervals and Table 8 uses 200 per run, distinct from the default 500. Table 2's trigger-token exclusion lowers activation while leaving substantial residual ASR. Clean-token add-back suppresses ASR when trigger tokens remain fixed. This supports a context-suppression mechanism in their tested LVLM. Their use of a fixed initial 576-token image representation does not by itself establish whether an absolute or fractional threshold governs activation when source length varies. [StripWake Fig. 1, §5.2 and Table 2, Appendix C Tables 7–8](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.01252v1)

## Evidence strength

Primary-source identity and distinctions in threat model, input conditions, and transformed state are direct. Repository counts and sample splits above are traceable to generated tables, summaries, and the evaluation code. The evidence supports a bounded study of intentional text-cache-conditioned behavior under a prefill proxy. Neither checked paper establishes the same trigger under the same capabilities. That is a comparison of two identified neighbors, and the broader first-of-kind claim remains a hypothesis.

## Counterevidence and alternative explanations

StripWake already demonstrates compressed-versus-full activation under an efficiency configuration, so deployment-conditioned activation alone is too broad a novelty claim. CacheTrap already demonstrates targeted text-model behavior under a transient KV cache perturbation, so the cache surface alone is too broad. Matched clean/benign and off-policy comparisons support trained selectivity on v2, while the v3 replacement marker demonstrates a deliberately trained output with sharp utility loss under activation. Metrics should stay conditional on their respective input types and cache conditions. The v2/v3 distinction also prevents interpreting cross-policy controls as direct evidence for all aspects of the v3 checkpoint.

## Open questions

- Do current papers outside these two neighbors establish the same text-only, input-unmodified, legitimate prefill eviction setting?
- Does the v3 headline persist under an independently verified physical prefill-eviction implementation on the headline model and split?
- Which manuscript numbers outside these checked sections use separate adapters or development sweeps and need explicit experiment labels?

## Recommended next action

Correct comparator wording throughout the manuscript, label v2 and v3 separately, cite the latest CacheTrap title, and rewrite the StripWake comparison around input trigger requirements and the state each runtime intervention changes. Bind the scientific claim to the static prefill setting, the synthetic replacement outcome, and the actual evaluation splits.
